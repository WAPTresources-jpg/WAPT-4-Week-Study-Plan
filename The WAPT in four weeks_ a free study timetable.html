How each day works

Spaced recall (30 min). Start with a blank page and write down everything you remember about the topics named, before opening any notes. It deliberately reaches back to older weeks, not just yesterday.

Two learning hours. Study the anatomy and physiology of the same system together. Read against the official objectives first, then use any textbook or lecture you like to fill gaps.

Practice and consolidation. An hour of X-type questions on the day's material from any source you have. If you have none, turn each objective into four or five true-or-false statements and test yourself on them. Then spend 30 minutes turning the day into one diagram or summary you could redraw from memory.

Revision and mocks

Every Saturday is a review day: blank-page recall of the whole week, weak-spot repair, and timed practice. Mocks fall on the Saturdays of weeks 2 and 3 and on the Friday of week 4, so each one leaves time to fix what it shows.

Each component is passed separately. Score Anatomy, Physiology and Molecular Medicine on their own after every mock. A strong score in one cannot carry a weak one, so shift Saturday time towards whichever is lowest.

In X-type questions every statement is judged on its own, and the number of true statements can vary. Practise deciding each one independently rather than looking for a pattern.

Before you start

The objectives define the syllabus. Download the current Anatomy, Physiology and Molecular Medicine objectives from the Wits GEMP page and check each topic off against them. Videos are a supplement and will sometimes go beyond what is examined.

Use whatever resources suit you. The lecture links simply open a YouTube search for the topic, so you can choose any clear explanation. A standard anatomy or physiology textbook works just as well.

Confirm your date and venue from your official Wits communication. The test is usually on a Monday, so this plan ends on the Sunday before. Enter your date above to fill in the calendar.
