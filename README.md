# The WAPT in 4 Weeks

A free, four-week study timetable for the **Wits Additional Placement Test (WAPT)**, the entry test for the Graduate Entry Medical Programme (GEMP) at the University of the Witwatersrand.

## Why this exists

I sat the WAPT in 2026 and passed. While preparing, I noticed how little structured, freely available guidance there is for this test. Most of what's out there is scattered across forums, group chats and paid courses, and it's hard to know where to start or how to pace yourself.

Before I wrote the test, I promised myself that if I passed, I'd put my study plan online for free. This is that promise kept.

The aim is simple: to help make good WAPT preparation available to everyone, not just those who can pay for it or happen to know the right people. This timetable is a starting point, and I hope it grows into something better with input from others who have been through the process.

## What's in the plan

- **4 weeks, 4 hours a day, Monday to Saturday**, with every Sunday off. That's 96 study hours in total.
- **All three components** are covered: Human Anatomy, Human Physiology and Molecular Medicine.
- **Anatomy and physiology of the same system are studied together**, so the structure and function reinforce each other.
- **Revision is built into every day**, through a 30-minute spaced-recall block that deliberately reaches back to earlier weeks.
- **Every Saturday is a review day**, with three timed mocks spread across the month.
- **The final weekend is deliberately light**, so you go into the test rested.

### A typical study day

| Time | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 30 min | Spaced recall | Blank-page recall of earlier topics before opening any notes |
| 60 min | Learning hour 1 | A system's anatomy or physiology, read against the objectives |
| 60 min | Learning hour 2 | The matching topic, or a Molecular Medicine thread |
| 60 min | Practice | X-type questions on the day's material |
| 30 min | Consolidation | One diagram or summary you could redraw from memory |

Short breaks sit between each block. The clock times on the timetable are only a suggestion, so shift them to suit your day.

## How to use it

1. **Download the official objectives first.** The current Anatomy, Physiology and Molecular Medicine objectives are on the [Wits GEMP page](https://www.wits.ac.za/health/gemp/). They define what's examined, so check every topic off against them.
2. **Count back four weeks from your test date.** Day 1 is the Monday four weeks before the test. The WAPT is usually written on a Monday, and the plan ends on the Sunday before.
3. **Use whatever resources suit you.** The plan doesn't depend on any particular textbook, course or question bank. Lecture links simply open a YouTube search for each topic.
4. **No question bank? Make your own.** Turn each objective into four or five true-or-false statements and test yourself on them. Doing this is useful revision in itself.
5. **Score each component separately after every mock.** Each component is passed separately, so a strong result in one can't carry a weak one. Shift your Saturday time towards whichever is lowest.

The web version lets you enter your test date to fill in the calendar and tick off sessions as you go. Your progress is saved in your own browser only.

## Important

- **This is not an official resource.** It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the University of the Witwatersrand.
- **Always confirm details with Wits.** Test dates, venues, format and objectives can change from year to year. Your official communication from Wits is the only binding source.
- **Four weeks is a compressed plan.** If you have more time, spread it out. If you have less, prioritise your weakest component.
